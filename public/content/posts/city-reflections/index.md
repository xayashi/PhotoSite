There's a version of your city you only see when you stop rushing through it. After months of winter, I started walking without a destination. Just me, a camera, and the long evening light of early June.

## Home Ground

I've lived here for years, but I'd stopped looking. The skyline from the Stone Arch Bridge. The way the river bends near the mill ruins. Light hitting a grain elevator at golden hour. All of it hiding in plain sight.

> "The real voyage of discovery consists not in seeking new landscapes, but in having new eyes."

## What I Found

Mostly, I found quiet. The city between rush hours has a rhythm that rewards patience. A cyclist crossing an empty bridge. Steam rising from a manhole at dawn. The geometry of parking ramps when no one's around.

This isn't a travel story. It's about paying attention to the place you already are.

## Process

I shot everything on a single prime lens. No zoom, no switching. The constraint forced me to move, to get closer, to find the frame with my feet instead of my hands. Some of the best work came from limitations I didn't choose.

The project isn't finished. I don't think it ever will be. That's the point.
